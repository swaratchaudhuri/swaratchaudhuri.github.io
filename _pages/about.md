Swarat Chaudhuri is a Professor of Computer Science at UT Austin and a Senior Staff Research Scientist at Google DeepMind, where he is currently on leave (Fall 2024&ndash;) with the Science and Strategic Initiatives Unit in London. His work is at the interface of programming languages, formal methods, and machine learning, and aims to build computing systems that are dependable, understandable, and inherently secure.

He leads the [Trishul](/) lab, whose research spans two threads. The first is **foundations**: program synthesis and automated reasoning, evolving from purely symbolic formal methods (Lambda2, ConSynth) towards neurosymbolic approaches that compose neural and symbolic computation (Houdini, DSE, Near), and — more recently — large language model agents for theorem proving and mathematical discovery (Copra, LaSR, CLEVER). The second is **applications**, organized around three themes: AI for code and mathematics; AI for systems, with an emphasis on interpretability and safety (Pirl, Propel, AI&sup2;); and AI for science, using neurosymbolic programs for interpretable, data-efficient discovery.

He received a bachelor's degree from the Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur (2001) and a PhD from the University of Pennsylvania (2007). His honors include the 2025 ACM Fellowship, a 2025 Guggenheim Fellowship, an NSF CAREER award, the ACM SIGPLAN John Reynolds Doctoral Dissertation Award, and the Morris and Dorothy Rubinoff Dissertation Award from the University of Pennsylvania, along with several distinguished paper awards. He was Program Chair for CAV 2016 and ICLR 2024.
